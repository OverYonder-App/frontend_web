# Go-to-market — OverYonder

**Defining fact:** network effects are *local*. Events, "friends going," and RSVPs
are all campus-scoped, so value comes from density on one campus — not breadth
across many. This drives the whole strategy.

## Beachhead: win one campus completely
Pick a single school where you have a foot in the door (College of Idaho appears
throughout the seed data — start there if it's a real relationship). Don't spread
thin. Make OverYonder *the* way students find events on that campus, then use it
as the reference case.

## Two motions, run together
- **Bottom-up (the wedge): club leaders.** Clubs need event promotion + attendance.
  Onboard 5–10 founding clubs; their members invite friends, and the "friends
  going" social proof + T-30/15/0 push reminders drive retention. This is the
  viral loop.
- **Top-down: Student Affairs / Campus Life / Student Government.** Sell them as the
  *official* campus events platform — they become the Institution Admin (approve
  events, post announcements, see analytics). This is who signs the check.

## Cold-start tactics
- Time launch to the **academic calendar** — orientation week + the fall club fair
  are the biggest acquisition moments. Miss them and you wait a semester.
- Recruit **RAs and student gov** as evangelists; table at the club fair; pre-seed
  the calendar with real events so day one isn't empty.

## Pricing
- **Always free for students.**
- **Institutions pay** (annual SaaS, tier by enrollment). Land the beachhead with a
  **free pilot semester**, convert on proven engagement. Paid features live on the
  admin side (analytics, SSO, announcements, branding) — students never paywalled.

## Metrics
- **North star:** weekly active students per campus.
- **Activation funnel:** signup → email confirmed → onboarded → joined a club /
  first RSVP.
- **Health:** events created/week, RSVP→attendance rate, W1/W4 retention.

## 90-day plan
- **Days 1–30:** deploy; onboard pilot campus admin; recruit + seed 10 clubs; blitz
  orientation / club fair.
- **Days 30–60:** drive weekly events; fix the top 3 friction points from real
  usage; measure activation + retention.
- **Days 60–90:** turn the pilot into a case study (students, events, attendance
  lift); line up 2–3 similar colleges; finalize pricing from real data.

## Risks
- **Long, calendar-gated institutional sales cycles** — plan around semesters.
- **Incumbents** (CampusGroups, Anthology Engage/Corq). Edge = student-first,
  social, modern UX — lean into "friends going" and reminders; don't out-feature
  the enterprise suites.
- **Data/privacy scrutiny** rises with institutional deals — privacy/terms pages
  and consent banner are a start; get counsel review + FERPA awareness before
  signing a school.
